# Visual cues inspectors look for

## Sidewall and tread cracking
Networks of fine cracks on the sidewall or in tread grooves (often called weather or ozone checking) indicate rubber ageing. Deep or widespread cracking that reaches the cords is a reason to remove a casing from service.

## Cuts, punctures and exposed cords
Cuts that expose fabric or steel cords, or penetrations through the crown, compromise the casing. Exposed cords are a serious defect.

## Bulges and separations
A bulge or blister on the sidewall or tread suggests internal separation or impact damage. Bulged casings should not be returned to service.

## Irregular wear
Uneven or patchy wear, flat spots and shoulder wear can point to alignment, inflation or loading problems. They matter for retreadability because the casing must still be structurally sound.

## Limits of a single photograph
One image shows one region of the tyre. The bead, inner liner and the far side are not visible, so a clean photo cannot clear a casing on its own. Capture several angles when in doubt.
