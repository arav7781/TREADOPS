# Retreading and casing circularity

## Why the casing matters
Retreading replaces the worn tread on a sound casing instead of manufacturing a new tyre, so most of the tyre's material is reused. Casing integrity therefore decides whether a tyre can enter a second or third life.

## Published Michelin figures (truck and bus)
Michelin states that one MICHELIN Remix retread needs about 50 kg less raw material than manufacturing a new tyre (a new MICHELIN truck tyre weighs about 70 kg on average), and calculates the raw-material saving at 2.3 kg of CO2 per kg of raw material, an internal cradle-to-gate LCA factor. A casing can be retreaded up to two times with MICHELIN Remix. Source: https://business.michelin.co.uk/help-advice/truck/tyre-basics-tyre-retreading

## How TreadOps uses these figures
TreadOps multiplies these published factors by inspector-verified retread dispositions only, giving an estimate of about 50 kg raw material and about 115 kg CO2e avoided per verified retread. Model recommendations are shown separately and never counted. The figures are estimates for truck and bus casings, not a measured life-cycle assessment of the tyres in this system.

## Pathways
Retread intake for sound casings, expert inspection when the evidence is uncertain, and end-of-life material recovery for casings that cannot be reused. Recovery routes for end-of-life tyres include material recycling (for example granulate) and recovery, handled by certified operators.
